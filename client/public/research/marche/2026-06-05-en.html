The Rate Shock

Nonfarm payrolls beat 172k (+100%). Markets instantly repriced the Fed hike odds to 70%. Everything fell together.

Consensus expected 80–85k jobs in May. The economy delivered 172k — more than double the forecast. The immediate read: strong labor market = the Fed doesn't have to cut. Markets flipped instantly. Fed fund futures repriced June hike odds from ~35% to 70%. The 10-year Treasury yield jumped from 4.45% to 4.55% — a +10 basis point daily move that has not happened in three months.

The paradox: a beat on jobs is normally bullish. Healthy labor market = company earnings grow. So why did the Nasdaq fall 4.68% and the S&P fall 2.59% on Friday alone? Because the good jobs data demolished the "soft landing" narrative. Markets had priced in steady growth with falling rates. This data suggested steady growth with rising rates. Rate shock trumped earnings optimism.

Nine-week winning streak ended. Nasdaq worst day since April 2025. Gold down 4.2%. No asset class had a safe harbor. That is not a correction — that is a repricing event.

The broader picture: equities had rallied for nine consecutive weeks into this Friday. The momentum was real. AI spending was real. But the repricing revealed the fragility underneath. Every asset sold off at the same time — stocks, bonds, gold. VIX jumped to 21.51 (+39.7% week). That simultaneous selling is the hallmark of a macro regime shift, not a sector rotation or a single stock story.

Why Markets Broke

Three forces hit simultaneously: rate shock, concentration risk in AI, and the collapse of traditional havens.

Driver 1: The Fed Repricing
Strong jobs mean the Fed might hike, not cut. Rate-sensitive names (mega-cap AI, unprofitable growth) repriced downward.

When the 10-year rate moves +10 bps in one day, discount rates on high-growth assets compress instantly. Nvidia, Broadcom, CrowdStrike, and the entire AI infrastructure complex derive their valuations from low-rate assumptions. This week's repricing hit that thesis directly. The rate shock matters more than the jobs beat for valuation markets.

Driver 2: Broadcom Silence Killed the AI Rally
The semiconductor rally ran on a belief that Broadcom would deliver record guidance. The miss collapsed AI momentum and revealed concentration risk.

Broadcom's financial guidance disappointed. The market had front-run an AI acceleration signal. The miss, combined with the rate shock, cascaded through the sector. The Nasdaq fell harder than the S&P because the index is heavily weighted to the very names that had driven May's gains. This week revealed a critical fragility: the rally was concentrated in a handful of picks, and when those picks reset, the whole index reprices.

Driver 3: No Safe Haven
Stocks fell. Bonds fell. Gold fell. Oil was the only winner. Diversification failed on a single day.

In a normal correction, investors flee to treasuries and gold. This week, both were down. The 10-year was down because rates spiked upward (price-inverse relationship). Gold fell 4.2% in a single day because the dollar strengthened on Fed repricing and real yields spiked. Oil was the one asset that rose (+3%) because geopolitical risk premium (Iran-Israel conflict) offsetting demand-destruction concerns. For portfolio managers, this meant no allocation cushion. Every rebalance trigger became a forced seller of the same assets.

Indian Ocean Signal

How a US rate shock transmits to Mauritius in weeks, not months. SEMDEX stayed flat. Rupee pressure mounted.

Mauritius was spared the Friday selloff — the SEMDEX was flat, isolated from the global repricing. But do not mistake calm for immunity. When US rates spike 10 bps in a single day, the yield spread between US and Mauritius assets compresses. This week, the gap between a 4.55% US 10-year and 5.87% MU 15-year narrowed from 142 bps to 132 bps. Capital flows to emerging markets slow. The rupee faces pressure as overseas investors rebalance back to higher-yielding US names.

The Mauritius PSA oil buffer delays pump-price pass-through by 4–6 weeks. But the macro signal is now: rate environment is tightening, not easing. Oil relief is coming, but only after the dollar-strength overhang fades.

⚠️ Oil Price Caveat

Mauritius oil prices at the pump are partially buffered by the PSA mechanism, creating a 2–4 week lag vs. global Brent prices. Today's $91 oil drop does not immediately translate to pump relief. Instead, it signals a softer rate environment and eases pressure on the PSA's currency buffer. The macro signal (easier money conditions) matters more than the consumer impact (which arrives later).

On oil specifically: WTI rose 3% this week on Iran-Israel escalation fears. But this is not consumer relief yet. The PSA mechanism delays retail pump-price changes by one full cycle. What matters is the underlying signal: higher US rates + stronger dollar typically compress commodity prices (including oil) within 4-6 weeks. The short-term geopolitical premium masks the medium-term rate headwind. Plan for oil to soften after the immediate tension fades, not before.

The Real Question
Is this a repricing on better information? Or the start of a longer regime shift?
One day of selling is volatility. One week is correction. But if the Fed actually hikes on June 16, and geopolitics stay tense, and AI concentration risk remains, then this is the beginning of a regime where "lower rates forever" is no longer the base case. That regime lasted from 2015–2021. A shift out of it doesn't happen in one week. It unfolds over 4-6 weeks. Watch the data. Watch the Fed. Watch the narrative around Warsh. The week of June 16 will tell you if this was a tactical repricing or a real regime change.
